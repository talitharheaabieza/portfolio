Thompson Sampling Ad Optimization (XYZ Coffee)

Jan - Apr 2026

Project Overview

This project explores real-time ad optimization using a Thompson Sampling multi-armed bandit algorithm. Traditional A/B testing splits traffic evenly throughout an experiment, wasting impressions on underperforming creatives. By leveraging Bayesian updating, Thompson Sampling dynamically shifts traffic toward winning variants while maintaining sufficient exploration to ensure statistical confidence.

Tested Ad Variants:

  • Variant A (Baseline): Static Image, No Discount
  • Variant B (Dynamic): Dynamic Visuals / Motion
  • Variant C (Discount): Promotional '20% OFF' Badge
  • Variant D (Dynamic & Discount): Combined Dynamic Visuals & Discount Badge

1. Traffic Allocation: Static A/B Test vs. Thompson Sampling

While traditional A/B testing splits impressions equally (25% per variant) regardless of performance, Thompson Sampling dynamically adjusts traffic based on real-time conversions. As the experiment progressed, the algorithm identified Variant A (Baseline) as the winning creative and automatically shifted 40% of overall traffic toward it, maximizing conversions while minimizing wasted impressions on underperforming variants.

Figure 1: Comparison of static equal split vs. adaptive Bayesian traffic allocation over time.

2. Click-Through Rate (CTR) by Ad Variant

Evaluating individual creative performance revealed that Variant A (Baseline) drove the highest engagement at 60% CTR. Surprisingly, adding visual enhancements or promotional badges decreased overall performance across the other test variants (Variant B - Dynamic: 36.36%, Variant C - Discount: 47.62%, Variant D - Dynamic & Discount: 50%).

Figure 2: Empirical Click-Through Rates across Baseline (A), Dynamic (B), Discount (C), and Combined (D) variants.

3. Statistical Analysis & Econometric Insights

To understand the root cause behind performance differences, a regression analysis was conducted on individual ad components:

Dynamic Element
Motion Hurts CTR
-0.24**

Adding dynamic visual elements reduced click-through rate by -0.24 (p<0.01). While movement grabbed visual attention, it ultimately lowered user click intent.

Discount Label
Discounts Depress Orders
-0.32*

The '20% OFF' badge reduced order likelihood by -0.32 (p<0.05). Price signaling inadvertently degraded perception of premium quality.

Combined Effect
Combined Adds Little
+0.26*

Combining dynamic visuals and discount badges resulted in a marginal uptick (+0.26, p<0.05), but the net effect remained negligible compared to the baseline.
