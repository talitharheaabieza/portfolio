Thompson Sampling Ad Optimization (XYZ Coffee)

Jan - Apr 2026

Project Overview

This project explores real-time ad optimization using a Thompson Sampling multi-armed bandit algorithm. Traditional A/B testing splits traffic evenly throughout an experiment, wasting impressions on underperforming creatives. By leveraging Bayesian updating, Thompson Sampling dynamically shifts traffic toward winning variants while maintaining sufficient exploration to ensure statistical confidence.

Tested Ad Variants:

  • Variant A (Baseline): Static Image, No Discount
  • Variant B (Dynamic): Dynamic Visuals / Motion
  • Variant C (Discount): Promotional '20% OFF' Badge
  • Variant D (Dynamic & Discount): Combined Dynamic Visuals & Discount Badge

1. Traffic Allocation: Static A/B Test vs. Thompson Sampling

While traditional A/B testing splits impressions equally (25% per variant) regardless of performance, Thompson Sampling dynamically adjusts traffic based on real-time conversions. As the experiment progressed, the algorithm identified Variant A (Baseline) as the winning creative and automatically shifted 40% of overall traffic toward it, maximizing conversions while minimizing wasted impressions on underperforming variants.

Figure 1: Comparison of static equal split vs. adaptive Bayesian traffic allocation over time.

2. Click-Through Rate (CTR) by Ad Variant

Evaluating individual creative performance revealed that Variant A (Baseline) drove the highest engagement at 60% CTR. Surprisingly, adding visual enhancements or promotional badges decreased overall performance across the other test variants (Variant B - Dynamic: 36.36%, Variant C - Discount: 47.62%, Variant D - Discount & Dynamic: 50%).

Figure 2: Empirical Click-Through Rates across Baseline (A), Dynamic (B), Discount (C), and Combined (D) variants.

3. Statistical Analysis & Econometric Insights

To understand the root cause behind performance differences, a regression analysis was conducted on individual ad components:

Dynamic Element
Motion Hurts CTR
-0.24**

Adding dynamic visual elements reduced click-through rate by -0.24 (p<0.01). While movement grabbed visual attention, it ultimately lowered user click intent.

Discount Label
Discounts Depress Orders
-0.32*

The '20% OFF' badge reduced order likelihood by -0.32 (p<0.05). Price signaling inadvertently degraded perception of premium quality.

Combined Effect
Combined Adds Little
+0.26*

Combining dynamic visuals and discount badges resulted in a marginal uptick (+0.26, p<0.05), but the net effect remained negligible compared to the baseline.

Market Basket Analysis (XYZ Restaurant, Indonesia)

Aug - Dec 2025

Project Overview

Conducted for a local Indonesian F&B restaurant chain using R to uncover product affinities across dayparts (noon vs. night) and design data-driven menu bundles using Association Rule Mining (Apriori Algorithm).

1. Hourly Sales Distribution

Analyzing transactional velocity across 24-hour cycles revealed two prominent sales peaks: a secondary lunch peak around 14:00 (60M+ Rp) and a primary dinner peak at 20:00 (80M+ Rp). This clear bimodal pattern informed the decision to segment association rules by daypart (Noon vs. Night).

Figure 1: Hourly transaction value highlighting distinct lunch and dinner operational peaks.

2. Daypart Rule Mining: Noon vs. Night Affinities

Association rule mining identified distinct consumer behaviors across meal times:

  • Noon Daypart (Mains + Non-Coffee): Strong affinity between savory mains like Fried Rice, Signature Fried Rice, or Spicy Chicken Steak with cold non-coffee beverages (Mineral Water, Lemon Tea, Matcha Blend). High confidence scores (>0.50) indicate these pairs are primary candidates for high-margin lunch bundles.
  • Night Daypart (Desserts + Coffee): High co-occurrence between sweet treats (Chocolate Cheese Fried Banana, Chocolate Cheese Toast) and specialty coffees (Signature Milk Coffee, Long Black). Rules exhibited strong lift values (>3.0), recommending evening dessert-and-coffee pairings.
Figure 2: Top 10 Product Association Rules Ranked by Weighted Score (Noon - Mains + Non-Coffee).
Figure 3: Top 10 Product Association Rules Ranked by Weighted Score (Night - Desserts + Coffee).

3. Menu Layout Optimization

Using weighted scoring, product category positions were optimized based on historical contribution and transaction volume. Categories like Non-Coffee and Coffee anchored the top slots (Sales Weight: 2.00 & 1.93), providing a structural template for menu redesign and promotional banners.

Figure 4: Weighted Scores and Actual Sales Volume for Menu Positioning (Noon).
