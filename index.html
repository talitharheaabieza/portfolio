Thompson Sampling Ad Optimization (XYZ Coffee)

Jan - Apr 2026

Project Overview

This project explores real-time ad optimization using a Thompson Sampling multi-armed bandit algorithm. Traditional A/B testing splits traffic evenly throughout an experiment, wasting impressions on underperforming creatives. By leveraging Bayesian updating, Thompson Sampling dynamically shifts traffic toward winning variants while maintaining sufficient exploration to ensure statistical confidence.

Tested Ad Variants:

  • Variant A (Baseline): Static Image, No Discount
  • Variant B (Dynamic): Dynamic Visuals / Motion
  • Variant C (Discount): Promotional '20% OFF' Badge
  • Variant D (Dynamic & Discount): Combined Dynamic Visuals & Discount Badge

1. Traffic Allocation: Static A/B Test vs. Thompson Sampling

While traditional A/B testing splits impressions equally (25% per variant) regardless of performance, Thompson Sampling dynamically adjusts traffic based on real-time conversions. As the experiment progressed, the algorithm identified Variant A (Baseline) as the winning creative and automatically shifted 40% of overall traffic toward it, maximizing conversions while minimizing wasted impressions on underperforming variants.

Figure 1: Comparison of static equal split vs. adaptive Bayesian traffic allocation over time.

2. Click-Through Rate (CTR) by Ad Variant

Evaluating individual creative performance revealed that Variant A (Baseline) drove the highest engagement at 60% CTR. Surprisingly, adding visual enhancements or promotional badges decreased overall performance across the other test variants (Variant B - Dynamic: 36.36%, Variant C - Discount: 47.62%, Variant D - Dynamic & Discount: 50%).

Figure 2: Empirical Click-Through Rates across Baseline (A), Dynamic (B), Discount (C), and Combined (D) variants.

3. Statistical Analysis & Econometric Insights

To understand the root cause behind performance differences, a regression analysis was conducted on individual ad components:

Dynamic Element
Motion Hurts CTR
-0.24**

Adding dynamic visual elements reduced click-through rate by -0.24 (p<0.01). While movement grabbed visual attention, it ultimately lowered user click intent.

Discount Label
Discounts Depress Orders
-0.32*

The '20% OFF' badge reduced order likelihood by -0.32 (p<0.05). Price signaling inadvertently degraded perception of premium quality.

Combined Effect
Combined Adds Little
+0.26*

Combining dynamic visuals and discount badges resulted in a marginal uptick (+0.26, p<0.05), but the net effect remained negligible compared to the baseline.

CRM Analysis: Missed Conversions & Churn (Duolingo)

Jan - Mar 2026

Project Overview

This project analyzed a simulated CRM database modeled after Duolingo's freemium subscription business, combining user profiles, email campaign logs, and subscription and payment records. The goal was to study two connected problems: users who engage heavily but never upgrade to a paid tier, and paying subscribers who cancel with little warning.

Using SQL to build engagement thresholds, cohort comparisons, and time-based churn windows, the analysis aimed to turn behavioral signals already sitting in the data into targeting rules a CRM team could act on.

1. Database Architecture

The underlying relational model links user profiles across campaign interactions, learning sessions, lesson attempts, active subscriptions, and payment history via user_id.

Entity Relationship Diagram (ERD) mapping the relational CRM schema.

2. Missed Conversions & Engagement Patterns

The strongest predictor of upgrading turned out to be sustained engagement, not short-term intensity. Users in the top XP quartile converted at roughly three times the rate of users in the bottom three quartiles, and a moderate cadence of about two sessions per week combined with a three to nine day streak produced the highest upgrade rates (72.9%).

Figure 1: Upgrade rates across XP Quartiles, Streak Lengths, and Weekly Session Frequencies.

Conversion also varied meaningfully by course and language pairing. English for Spanish Speakers converted at 41.2%, compared to 27.9% for Turkish for English Speakers, suggesting premium messaging should be tailored by course rather than applied uniformly.

Figure 2: Conversion rate benchmarks broken down by language pairing.

A separate check on email-engaged users found that opening or clicking a campaign email was a weak signal on its own. Of 2,634 engaged users, only 241 converted (~91% non-conversion rate), with acquisition channel and device combination explaining more of the gap than region did.

3. Surprise Churn Dynamics

Cancellations were rarely sudden. Users who eventually churned showed measurably lower session counts, active days, and XP in the 7, 14, and 30-day windows before cancellation compared to retained users. Even within the churned group, engagement dipped further in the final week relative to the week prior.

Plan tier and auto-renew settings also played a key role: Super plan subscribers with auto-renew turned off churned at 46.5%—representing the largest recoverable segment in the dataset—while Max plan users churned fastest overall (canceling after an average of ~29 days vs. ~219 days for Family plan subscribers).

Figure 3: Churn rates across Family, Max, and Super tiers segmented by auto-renew status.

Strategic Recommendations

  • Segmented Upgrade Prompts: Trigger personalized upgrade prompts for the high-XP, moderate-activity segment rather than messaging all engaged users equally.
  • Course-Specific Paywalls: Prioritize paywall placements around the strongest converting course/language pairs while investigating user friction in weaker performing courses.
  • Win-Back & Retention Campaigns: Run targeted auto-renew recovery campaigns aimed at Super plan users with auto-renew switched off, utilizing early engagement drops as an intervention window prior to cancellation.

Market Basket Analysis: Data-Driven Bundling Strategy (XYZ Restaurant)

Aug - Dec 2025 · Course Project for 'Big Data' (NUS MSc MAI)

Project Overview

As part of the 'Big Data' course in the MSc MAI program at NUS, this market basket analysis was conducted for a local Indonesian F&B restaurant chain (client identity masked for confidentiality), using R to identify product and category affinities and inform bundling strategy.

Key Findings & Time-Period Patterns

Analyzed purchase patterns across time periods (noon vs. night) to uncover distinct consumption behavior:

  • Noon Consumption: Skewed toward functional, balanced meal-drink pairings (e.g., Mains & Non-Coffee).
  • Night Consumption: Skewed toward indulgent, leisure-oriented pairings (e.g., Desserts & Coffee).

Top Identified Bundles:

  • Noon Signature Bundle ("Day Combo"): Fried Rice + Mineral Water
  • Night Signature Bundle ("Evening Treat"): Chocolate Cheese Fried Banana + Coffee Latte

Actionable Recommendations

Menu Layout
Time-Period Design

Structure menus differently by daypart, leading with functional pairings at noon and indulgent pairings at night.

Bundling
Signature Combos

Package top affinity pairs into named combos like "Day Combo" and "Evening Treat" for seamless ordering.

Placement
Cross-Selling

Position high-affinity categories close to each other on menus and POS systems to drive add-on orders.

Consumer Perception: Hawker Centres vs Modern Dining in Singapore

Aug 2025 – Nov 2025 · Research for Marketing Insights (NUS MSc MAI)

Project Overview

This project examined why youth in Singapore choose hawker centers over modern dining options (cafes, food courts, restaurants), and how digital platforms shape trial and perception.

Research Methodology

A mixed-methods design combining quantitative and qualitative approaches:

  • Quantitative Survey: 70 valid responses analyzed using RStudio.
  • R Analytics: Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA), Linear Regression, and K-Means Cluster Analysis.
  • Qualitative Research: Focus Group Discussion (5 participants) with thematic sentiment analysis on qualitative responses.

Key Findings

Core Value Drivers
Hawker Advantages

Affordability, convenience, and cultural identity emerged as hawker centres' strongest value propositions, while hygiene and ambience remained key weaknesses.

Factor Analysis
3 Perceptual Dimensions

Identified 3 main dimensions, with "Functional & Utility" being the only statistically significant predictor of hawker preference.

Cluster Analysis
3 Consumer Segments

Revealed distinct behavioral profiles, including a highly engaged segment of young, frequent hawker diners.

Strategic Recommendations

  • Short-Term: Focus on functional convenience and digital visibility (e.g., live crowd tracking, digital payment integration).
  • Long-Term: Target hygiene and ambience upgrades alongside tailored marketing strategies to engage less-frequent users.

Run Rate Forecasting Model Refinement

Jan 2023 – Apr 2024

Problem Statement

The existing run-rate calculation relied on a simple linear projection:

(Total Days in Month ÷ Days Elapsed) × Performance-to-Date

This baseline calculation failed to capture weekday vs. weekend traffic drops and e-commerce promotional cycles, resulting in a large projected-vs-actual variance of 20% to 30%.

Key Logic & Enhancements

  • Day-of-Week Seasonality: Factored in daily shopping velocity variations (weekdays vs. weekends) across product categories.
  • Monthly & Campaign Patterns: Incorporated historic campaign spike ratios (e.g., pay-day sales, double-digit mega campaigns).
  • Weighted Scoring: Applied exponentially weighted moving averages to prioritize recent daily performance trends over distant historical averages.

Business Impact

Accuracy
Reduced Variance
5% – 10%

Dramatically narrowed the gap between projected and actual month-end figures (down from 20–30%).
